I have analyzed your entire source code. Here is exactly what is happening and the direction you should take to solve it (explained simply, without writing code):
1. The "Scanner vs. Generator" Gap
In your app, the Generator is like a chef who can make 35 different dishes. But the Scanner/Details page is like a customer who only recognizes a few types of food by looking at them.
The Issue: When you generate a QR for an "Event Ticket" or "Table Menu," the app creates a simple Link (URL). When you scan it later, the scanner sees a URL and thinks, "Oh, this is just a website," so it shows a basic "Website" card instead of the beautiful "Ticket" card you saw in the generator.
The Solution: You need to save "Metadata." When a user generates a QR using a template, the app should save the template_id in your database. When the scanner opens that QR, it should look up the ID and say, "Aha! This isn't just a link; it's a 'Table Order' template," and then show the matching UI.
2. Why the "Click Button" isn't working
I found a specific setting in your code that is likely the culprit.
The Issue: In your QrDetailScreen.tsx, there is a line that says: "Hide the open button if the user is not logged in."
Why it's annoying: If you are testing the app as a "Guest" (not signed in), the big action button (like "Open Link" or "Pay Now") will be hidden or disabled.
The Solution: You should let guests open links! The "Social" features (voting, reporting) should require a login, but the basic functionality of "Scanning and Opening" should work for everyone.
3. UI Design Mismatch
The Generator and the QR Details page use two different sets of code to show how a "Payment" or "WhatsApp" card looks.
The Issue: You have designed beautiful layouts in the Generator preview, but the ContentCard in the Details page has its own separate, older design logic.
The Solution: You need to create Shared Components. Instead of having two different codes for a "WhatsApp Card," you should have one piece of code that both the Generator and the Detail page use. This ensures they always look identical.
4. Direction: How to make the idea work
You mentioned feeling directionless about the "QR Guard" idea. Here is how you make this app valuable in a market like India:
Trust as the Product: In India, many people are afraid of "QR Scams" (UPI scams). Your app's main power is the Trust Score.
Branded Templates: When a shopkeeper generates a "Merchant UPI" QR using your app, your app should "Sign" it. When a customer scans it, the app should show a "Verified by QR Guard" badge. This is your "Blue Tick."
The "Community" Hook: You are already starting to build a "QR Social Network." Let users see which QR codes are trusted by their friends or local community. This is why you couldn't "locate users"—the connection between scanning and social trust isn't strong enough yet.
Summary of what to focus on next:
Make "Opening" work for Guests: Stop hiding the button if someone isn't logged in.
Identify Templates: When saving a QR, make sure the templateId is stored so the scanner knows what it's looking at.
Sync the Design: Pick your favorite design (either from the Generator or the Details) and make it the "Single Truth" for the whole app.
Don't be overwhelmed. You have built a massive foundation. You just need to "wire" the pieces together so they share the same information!
